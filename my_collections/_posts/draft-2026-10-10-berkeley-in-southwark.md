### Third Berkeley scheme rejected 

Southwark Council unanimously rejected developer Berkeley's proposals for the development of Bermondsey Place Phases 2 and 3, at its planning committtee meeting on 6 October.  This follows Berkeley's failure to gain approval for its proposals for the Borough Triangle, at the [beginning of September](https://moderngov.southwark.gov.uk/ieListDocuments.aspx?CId=650&MId=8449&Ver=4) and the Aylesham Centre, [in July 2025](https://35percent.org/2025/07/16/aylesham-centre-planning-application-rejected.html).

Berkeley pre-empted Southwark's refusal of their Aylesham Centre proposals, by appealing to the Secretary of State for Housing, occasioning a nine-day public Inquiry.  This also [rejected the Aylesham Application](https://35percent.org/2026/06/08/aylesham-centre-decision-the-backlash.html).  The final outcome of a further appeal to the High Court is still awaited.  As yet there is no word as to whether Berkeley will appeal against the Borough Triangle decision.

All three applications entailed big reductions in affordable housing, either by way of a s73 variation of an existing planning permission (Borough Triangle), by securing a whole new planning permission (Bermondsey Place) or by amending the planning application before it had been determined (Aylesham Centre).  Had all three revisions succeeded it would have reduced the number of affordable homes from XXXXX  to YYYYY.  The number of social rented homes would have been reduced by ZZZZZ to WWWWWW.

The reasons for refusal in each case have been different.  The Aylesham Centre was refused by the Inpector because of heritage harm, though Southwark's planning committee added......Borough Triangle was refused on the grounds that.........
Bermondsey Place was refused because of.....

In every case affordable housing was a major point of contention.  The Aylesham Inspector was satisfied that the 12% affordable housing offer was the maximum reasonable.  Southwark Council's three decisions, though, all cite the low level of affordable housing a reason for refusing the applications [REFS].

Berkeley's arguments for all three sites were broadly the same - that economic circumstances, including rising build and finance costs, slow sales and new regulatory requirements had rendered their schemes unviable and unable to support the 35% affordable housing required by the Southwark Plan (London Plan?).
